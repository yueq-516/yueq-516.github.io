My name is **Yue Qin** and I am a PhD candidate of the <a href="https://www.sci.pitt.edu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>School of Computing and Information</strong></a> at the **University of Pittsburgh**.

My research focuses on leveraging ***machine learning*** and ***large language models (LLMs)*** to inform **flood preparedness** in **urban navigation** (<a href="https://calendar.pitt.edu/event/an-ai-driven-framework-of-flood-preparedness-in-urban-navigation" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">proposal talk</a>). More specifically, I am interested in (1) gathering flood-related knowledge from **open data** to facilitate navigation decision-making among multiple stakeholders, (2) building **flood prediction models** from open data to distinguish **flood occurrence** from **flood observations**, and (3) developing a decision-support system using **multi-agent LLMs** to help people better prepare for travel disruptions and answer questions such as "*which route should be taken to avoid potential flooding if heavy rain is forecasted*". It is essential, yet surprisingly difficult, to find answers to questions as simple as these, given all the techniques and tools at our disposal.

More broadly, I am interested in **spatial cognition** and **spatiotemporal modeling** to understand **urban navigation** and **mobility**. I genuienly believe how we perceive, process, store, and utilize spatial information is fundamental to how we interact with our environment.
