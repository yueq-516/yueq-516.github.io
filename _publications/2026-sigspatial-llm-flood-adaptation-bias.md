Large language models (LLMs) have been increasingly adopted
for simulating individual travel behaviors and supporting spatial
decision-making during mobility disruptions caused by natural hazards. However, it remains unknown whether the generated hazard
adaptations differ among individuals with different levels of socioeconomic status (SES). Building on the definition of socioeconomic
bias in the context of hazard adaptation generation, which is the
extent to which the generated recommendations and simulations
vary among personas with distinct socioeconomic attributes, we
systematically evaluate six LLMs using an adaptation ranking task.
Statistical testing on the metrics of list-wise differences between
high- and low-SES reveals significant effects of prompting strategies
and system roles, while linear regression models illustrate positive
effects of zero-shot prompting and explicit prohibition of candidate
position usage in mitigating bias. The results also demonstrate the
importance of mobility context, including how flood risk of alternative routes and trip purposes affect the level of socioeconomic
bias under different trip conditions. Our findings provide important
evidence on the existence of socioeconomic bias in hazard adaptation generation, informing context-based and equitable adoption
of LLMs in downstream applications for spatial decision-support.
