As urban floods become more frequent and damaging, understanding when and where floods will occur is crucial for risk management and mitigation. Most urban flood models adopting observations from physical or social sensors can suffer from limited spatial transferability or biased reporting in crowdsourcing, while the lack of publicly available data on flood occurrences makes predictions at the street segment-level remain challenging. To address these gaps, we collect and publish two open datasets on street-level flood events from open records for the city of [Pittsburgh](https://zenodo.org/records/17562033) and [New York City](https://zenodo.org/records/18062104), and propose a latent-state urban flood prediction model that distinguishes between flood generation and flood observation while taking into account heterogeneous reporting behaviors among different demographics.

Using the NYC dataset as a case study, we show during evaluation that our proposed model can predict floods observed by physical sensors and crowdsourcing, while providing interpretable results on how census demographics are associated with reporting biases. Furthermore, the model allows inference of the flooding status of the citywide street network before, during, and immediately after extreme storm events. All results are crucial for decision-makers from multiple stakeholders to improve the equitability and efficiency of resource allocation in flood mitigation, preparedness, and response.
